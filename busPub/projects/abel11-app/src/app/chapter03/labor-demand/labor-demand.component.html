The Determination of Labor Demand

  • The amount of labor demanded at any given real wage rate is the quantity of labor at which the marginal product of labor (MPN) equals the real wage rate.
  • Use the slider to see how the quantity of labor demanded changes with the changes in the real wage, .
  • As the real wage decreases, the quantity of labor demanded increases.

Factors that Shift the Labor Demand Curve

  • A beneficial supply shock, or an increase in the capital stock, that raises the MPN at every level of labor shifts the labor demand curve (ND) upward and to the right.
  • An adverse supply shock, or a decrease in the capital stock, that lowers the MPN at every level of labor shifts the labor demand curve (ND) downward and to the left.
  • Use the sliders below to simulate these shocks.
  • Moving the productivity slider to the left simulates an adverse supply shock and moving the slider to the right simulates a beneficial supply shock
  • Moving the capital stock slider to the left simulates a decline in the capital stock and moving the slider to the right simulates an increase in the capital stock.

The Determination of Labor Supply

  • The amount of labor supplied depends the real wage.
  • Use the slider to see how the quantity of labor supplied changes with the changes in the real wage, .
  • As the real wage increases, the quantity of labor supplied increases.

Factors that Shift the Labor Supply Curve

  • Each of the following changes reduces the quantity of labor supplied at any given real wage rate and thus shifts the labor supply curve to left:
  • An increase in wealth
  • An increase in the expected future real wage rate
  • A decrease in working-age population
  • A decrease in the labor force participation rate

Labor Market Equilibrium

  • In equilibrium, the real wage equals the value at which the quantity of labor demanded equals the quantity of labor supplied.

How Shifts in Labor Supply and Labor Demand Affect Equilibrium in the Labor Market

  • Move the "Productivity" slider to the left to simulate a temporary adverse supply shock that affects the location of the labor demand curve.
  • Notice that the equilibrium real wage rate falls and the equilibrium level of employment falls.
  • Move the “Wealth” slider to the right to simulate an increase in wealth. Notice that the equilibrium real wage rate increases and the equilibrium level of employment falls.
