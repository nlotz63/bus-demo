Saving Curve

  • Desired national saving is the level of national saving that occurs when aggregate consumption is at its desired level.
  • The saving curve, S, shows the level of desired national saving at each real interest rate.
  • Desired national saving increases when the real interest rate increases, so the saving curve slopes upward.

Investment Curve

  • Desired investment is the level of investment that firms desire to reach their desired capital stock.
  • The investment curve, I, shows the amount that firms want to invest in new capital goods at each real interest rate.
  • The investment curve slopes downward because a higher real interest rate raises the user cost of capital and thus lowers the amount of capital that firms want to use.

Equilibrium

  • Goods market equilibrium requires that desired national saving equal desired investment, or .
  • Goods market equilibrium occurs in the diagram at the point where the saving curve and investment curve intersect.
  • At the equilibrium point, desired national saving equals desired investment.
  • The equilibrium real interest rate is the real interest rate that clears the goods market.

Factors that Shift Saving

  • An increase in current output causes desired national saving to rise because part of the extra income is saved to provide for future consumption.
  • An increase in expected future output causes desired national saving to fall because anticipation of future income raises current desired consumption, lowering current desired saving.
  • An increase in wealth causes desired national saving to fall because some of the extra wealth is consumed, which reduces saving for given income.

Factors that Shift Investment

  • An increase in government purchases causes desired national saving to fall because higher government purchases directly lower desired national saving.
  • An increase in taxes causes desired national saving to remain unchanged or rise. Saving doesn't change if consumers take into account an offsetting future tax cut; saving rises if consumers don't take into account a future tax cut and thus reduce current consumption.

Factors that Shift Investment

  • An increase in the effective tax rate causes desired investment to fall because the tax-adjusted user cost increases, which reduces desired capital stock.
  • An increase in expected future MPK causes desired investment to rise because the desired capital stock increases.
