Overview of the Model

  • The figure consists of two saving-investment diagrams, one for the home country and one for the foreign country.
  • The world real interest rate, measured on the vertical axis, is the real interest rate faced by both countries.
  • The world real interest rate adjusts to achieve equilibrium in the goods market, such that one country's desired international lending equals the other countr's desired international borrowing.

Goods Market Equilibrium

  • To restore general equilibrium, change the world real interest rate so that desired lending in the home country equals desired borrowing in the foreign country. Use the spinner to fine tune your input.
  • Note that as the world real interest rate rises, desired lending in the home country increases and desired borrowing in the foreign country declines.

Changes in Desired Saving or Desired Investment (Home)

  • The home country's desired saving curve shifts up and to the left if wealth increases.
  • The home country's desired investment curve shifts down and to the left if the effective tax rate on capital increases.
  • Change the level of either wealth or the effective tax rate on capital to see what happens.
