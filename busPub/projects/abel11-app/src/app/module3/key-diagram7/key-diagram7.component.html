Overview of the AD/AS model

  • The real interest rate, , is on the vertical axis, and output, , is on the horizontal axis.
  • The full-employment line, , is vertical at full-employment output.
  • For any level of output, , the IS curve gives the real interest rate, \(r\), that clears the goods market.
  • For given values of the price level and output, the LM curve gives the real interest rate that clears the asset market.
  • The economy is in general equilibrium at the intersection of all three curves.

Factors that shift the AD curve: IS curve shifters (part 1)

  • The IS curve shifts up and to the right because of a decline in desired national saving if
    • Wealth increases,
    • Expected future output increases,
    • Government purchases increase,
    • Taxes decrease.

Factors that shift the AD curve: IS curve shifters (part 2)

  • The IS curve shifts up and to the right because of an increase in desired investment if
    • Expected future TFP increases,
    • The effective tax rate on capital decreases.

Factors that shift the AD curve: LM curve shfiters (part 3)

  • The LM curve shifts down and to the right because of an increase in the real money supply if
    • Nominal money supply increases,
    • Price level decreases.
  • The LM curve shifts down and to the right because of a decrease in real money demand if
    • Expected inflation increases,
    • Nominal interest rate on monetary assets decreases.

Factors that shift the SRAS curve

  • If the LM curve shifts down and to the right because of a shock, the price level will rise, shifting the LM curve up and to the left to restore general equilibrium.
  • If the IS curve shifts up and to the right because of a shock, the price level will rise, shifting the LM curve up and to the left to restore general equilibrium.
  • If the FE line shifts to the right because of a shock, the price level will decline, shifting the LM curve down and to the right to restore general equilibrium.

Factors that shift the LRAS curve

  • The FE line shifts to the right because of an increase in the full-employment level of output caused by
    • A beneficial supply shock that causes an increase in total factor productivity,
    • An increase in labor supply,
    • An increase in the capital stock.
