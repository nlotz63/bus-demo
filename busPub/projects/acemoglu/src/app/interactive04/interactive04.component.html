Key Points:

  • A variable cost is the cost of variable factors of production, which change along with a firm's output.
  • Average variable cost, average total cost, and marginal cost are all affected by changes in the price of variable factors.
  • A fixed cost is the cost of fixed factors of production, which a firm must pay even if it produces zero output.
  • Only average total cost is affected by changes in the cost of a fixed factor. It's worth noting that the average fixed cost is the vertical distance between average total cost and average variable cost.
  • Use the sliders to see the effect of changes in fixed and variable costs on the cost curves.

Key Points:

  • Profit is maximized (or the loss minimized) by producing where marginal revenue (MR) equals marginal cost (MC).
  • In a competitive market, marginal revenue is equal to the market price.
  • When marginal revenue is greater than marginal cost, the last unit produced adds to profit.
  • When marginal revenue is less than marginal cost, the last unit produced subtracted from profit.
