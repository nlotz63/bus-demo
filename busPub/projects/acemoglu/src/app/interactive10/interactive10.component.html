Externalities

Key Points:


Negative externality Positive externality
  • For a price ceiling to be binding, it must be set below the market clearing price.
  • Although a price ceiling can increase consumer surplus, it results in a deadweight loss and a decrease in producer surplus.
  • At the price ceiling, the quantity demanded is greater than the quantity supplied; therefore, less than the efficient quantity of apartments is being rented. This is the cause of the deadweight loss.
  • In the presence of price ceilings, there is tendency for illicit market to form because potential renters are willing to pay higher rents.
  • For a price floor to be binding, it must be set above the market clearing price.
  • Although a price floor increases producer surplus, it results in a deadweight loss and a decrease in consumer surplus.
  • At the price floor, the quantity supplied is greater than the quantity demanded; therefore, less than the efficient quantity of cheese is being purchased. This is the cause of the deadweight loss.
